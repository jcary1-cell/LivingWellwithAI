Chapter 1
Start here

So what is AI, really?

Think of today's AI chat tools as a very well-read assistant who has never left the library. It has read an enormous amount of writing and learned how words usually fit together. It's quick and helpful, and sometimes confidently wrong.

Predicts

It guesses the next word

When you ask a question, it builds an answer one word at a time, picking what's most likely to come next. That's why it sounds so natural. It's also why it can make things up without knowing it.

Learns

From patterns, not experience

It learned from books, websites and articles. It has never tasted barbecue or watched a sunrise. It knows about life the way a library knows about life.

Assists

You stay in charge

The best way to use it is as a helper. It drafts, explains and suggests. You decide, check and finish. Your judgment and life experience are the part it can't supply.

Where AI shines

  • Explaining a confusing letter or form in plain words
  • Drafting a note, toast, or thank-you you can then make your own
  • Brainstorming gift ideas, menus, or things to do
  • Answering “how do I…” questions about your phone or computer
  • Being patient with the same question asked five different ways

Where to be careful

  • Facts, dates, quotes and phone numbers. Always double-check.
  • Medical, legal or money decisions. Use it to prepare questions, then ask a professional.
  • Recent news. It may not know what happened this week.
  • Anything private. Treat the chat box like a postcard, not a sealed letter.
  • Voices, photos and videos online. They can now be faked convincingly.

Which tool?

You don't need anything fancy

Most of these have a free version and work on a phone, tablet or computer. Pick one and get comfortable before trying others.

Chat assistants
ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude and Microsoft Copilot. You type or speak a question and get a written answer.
Voice assistants
Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant. Good for timers, reminders, weather and quick questions, hands-free.
Seeing helpers
Apps like Be My Eyes and Seeing AI can describe what your camera sees or read printed text out loud.
Built in already
Your phone's photo search, email spam filter and map directions all use AI quietly in the background.

Practice

How to ask so you get a good answer

Talk to it the way you'd brief a helpful new neighbor. Say what you want, give a little background, and say how you'd like the answer. Build one below and watch it come together.

1What would you like help with?
4How would you like the answer?

Your request, ready to use

Why this works: it says the job, gives context only you could provide, and tells the assistant how to shape the answer. If you don't like what comes back, just reply “Try again, but shorter” or “Make it less formal.” You can keep going back and forth as long as you like.

  • Ask follow-ups

    The first answer is a draft. Say “simpler, please” or “give me three options.”

  • Check the facts

    Ask “Where does that come from?” Then look it up somewhere you trust.

  • Keep it general

    Say “my bank,” not the name and account number. It doesn't need specifics to help.

  • Make it yours

    Edit what it writes so it sounds like you. Your voice is the point.

Chapter 4
Stay safe

Privacy, security, and spotting fakes

AI makes good things easier. Unfortunately it makes scams easier too. A few simple habits protect you from nearly all of it.

Never type these into an AI chat

  • Social Security numberOr your Medicare ID. No helper ever needs them.
  • Bank and card numbersAsk about “my checking account” in general terms.
  • Passwords and PINsNot even to ask whether a password is strong.
  • Full medical recordsDescribe a symptom or question instead of pasting a chart.
  • Other people's private detailsYour neighbor's diagnosis is their story to share.
  • Photos of ID or checksDriver's license, passport or a signed check.

Real or scam? Try five.

The voice-clone call

  1. Pick a family safe word. Something only your family knows. Anyone calling in a “crisis” has to say it.
  2. Hang up and call back on the number you already have saved, not the one that called you.
  3. Slow down. Scammers rely on panic. Real emergencies can wait five minutes for you to check.
  4. Gift cards, wire transfers, crypto: nobody legitimate asks to be paid this way. Ever.

Tidy up your privacy settings

  • In any AI app, open Settings and look for words like “Data controls,” “Activity,” or “Improve the model.” You can usually turn off having your chats used for training.
  • Delete old chats you no longer need.
  • Use a strong, unique password and turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Only download apps from the official App Store or Google Play.

If something feels off, tell someone

Talking it over with a neighbor or family member is the best scam filter there is. And it's never embarrassing. These schemes fool smart people every day.

Chapter 5
Perspectives

One tool, many points of view

Good people disagree about AI, and that's healthy. Step into a few different shoes. Each has something true to say, and a fair question worth asking.

Five principles for working with AI responsibly

You're the editor

AI drafts. You decide what's true, kind and worth sending.

Be honest about it

If AI helped write something important, it's fine to say so.

Protect others too

Don't share friends' photos, names or stories without asking.

Question the answer

Ask who might be left out, or what it might have gotten wrong.

Keep people central

Use it to free up time for each other, not to replace each other.

The planet

What does a question to AI cost the Earth?

AI runs in giant buildings full of computers called data centers. They use electricity and water to stay cool, including some right here in the Carolinas. Here's what Google reports in its 2026 Environmental Report, and how to think about it.

Reading this with a fair eye

  • These are Google's own numbers about its own products. They're detailed and useful, but it's a company report, not an independent audit.
  • Both things are true: each question is tiny, and the total is growing fast because billions of people are asking.
  • The report itself says reaching its climate goals is “getting harder.” That candor is worth noticing.
  • Closer to home, Google notes data centers in the Carolinas and work with local partners on energy costs and home weatherization.

Mindful habits that add up

  • Ask clear, complete questions. Fewer do-overs means less energy.
  • Use AI where it truly helps, not out of habit for things a quick search or a neighbor could answer.
  • Go easy on making AI pictures and videos just for fun. They use much more energy than text.
  • Keep your devices longer. Making new gadgets is a big part of tech's footprint.
